### Discussion

The most obvious point about how costs change over time is the drop in costs when the mortgage is paid off.

The other notable point is how replacement reserves gradually dominate costs.

On the income side it is notable how big the profits get.

For a 25 year amortization period if we look at the folowing table of Net Present Value (NPV) for each assumed interest rate we note that the total ranges from about $1,250,000 to about $150,000 dollars for a $60,000 investment, depending on a (reasonable) interet rate assumption.

The Internal Rate of Return (IRR) (the interest rate that drops the NPV to zero) is about 15%.  This is about twice the expected rate of a stock index.

For a 50 year amortization period the returns are somewhat lower, although the IRR is close, this woould suggest that profitability has a sensitive dependence on IRR.

These should be considered lower bounds on the rate of return because of the conservative rent increases in the model.
